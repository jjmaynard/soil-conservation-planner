# Field Analysis Tab Narratives

## Purpose
This guide provides communication-ready narratives for each tab in the Field Analysis module.

Each tab narrative includes:
- What information the tab provides
- How to interpret it correctly
- What decision support value it offers
- A practical example relevant to farmers, land managers, and field scientists

## Soil Composition
- What this tab shows: A spatial summary of soil map units and core properties such as texture, organic matter, depth, hydrologic group, and drainage class from SSURGO.
- How to interpret: Use this as the baseline condition layer. Compare dominant and minor components to identify where constraints may appear first.
- Decision support value: Helps set realistic crop plans, tillage intensity, and amendment priorities by zone instead of relying on whole-field averages.
- Practical example: A producer identifies shallow, lower-organic-matter inclusions in one corner and shifts that area to a lower-risk crop while targeting organic amendments there.

## Erosion Risk
- What this tab shows: Estimated erosion risk and soil loss patterns from terrain, slope, and erosivity indicators.
- How to interpret: High-risk zones are intervention priorities, especially where slope and concentrated flow overlap.
- Decision support value: Supports conservation planning, compliance documentation, and practice selection such as contouring, reduced tillage, strip cropping, or cover placement.
- Practical example: Before fall tillage, a planner identifies a severe-risk shoulder slope and recommends no-till plus cover crops on that zone.

## Drainage
- What this tab shows: Drainage class, ponding tendency, depressional areas, and wetness indicators.
- How to interpret: Persistent wet signatures indicate delayed trafficability and root stress risk.
- Decision support value: Guides drainage improvements, controlled traffic decisions, and operation timing to reduce compaction and stand loss.
- Practical example: A manager sees repeated ponding in one swale and prioritizes targeted tile repair and later planting in that zone.

## Productivity
- What this tab shows: Productivity potential and performance signals including NCCPI context and NDVI-based multi-year patterns.
- How to interpret: Persistent yield gaps suggest structural limits; unstable performance suggests weather or water sensitivity.
- Decision support value: Supports variable-rate inputs, variety placement, and ROI-focused management strategies by zone.
- Practical example: An agronomist separates stable-low and unstable-low areas, then applies lower-input strategy to one and drainage-focused interventions to the other.

## Soil Vulnerability
- What this tab shows: Surface and subsurface loss vulnerability potential through the Soil Vulnerability Index.
- How to interpret: Higher values indicate greater nutrient and sediment transport risk under runoff or drainage.
- Decision support value: Prioritizes nutrient stewardship and edge-of-field conservation where loss potential is intrinsically higher.
- Practical example: A planner uses high subsurface vulnerability areas to prioritize controlled drainage and nutrient timing restrictions.

## Concentrated Flow
- What this tab shows: Concentrated flow pathways and gully-prone corridors from accumulation and terrain energy metrics.
- How to interpret: Linear high-intensity corridors indicate likely episodic damage pathways during large rainfall events.
- Decision support value: Supports placement of grassed waterways, diversions, and stabilization practices.
- Practical example: A land manager overlays known washout points with mapped flow paths to prioritize one high-risk corridor for immediate treatment.

## Drought Risk
- What this tab shows: Moisture stress indicators including water balance and drought index context.
- How to interpret: Negative water balance with declining drought index values indicates rising stress exposure.
- Decision support value: Supports irrigation prioritization, drought-tolerant crop choices, and contingency planning.
- Practical example: A producer with limited irrigation capacity allocates water to the most responsive acres and shifts marginal acres to a drought-resilient option.

## Vegetation Monitoring
- What this tab shows: Vegetation vigor and temporal NDVI dynamics, including repeat stress signatures.
- How to interpret: Recurring weak-growth zones suggest structural constraints; isolated dips may be weather events.
- Decision support value: Enables targeted scouting, post-season diagnosis, and location-specific remediation.
- Practical example: A consultant identifies a recurring low-vigor strip, confirms compaction, and recommends targeted subsoiling only in that strip.

## Terrain Attributes
- What this tab shows: Topographic controls such as slope position, convergence, and moisture redistribution patterns.
- How to interpret: Terrain context explains why similar soils behave differently across the same field.
- Decision support value: Improves siting of practices, equipment routing, and terrain-aware zone planning.
- Practical example: A field scientist redesigns traffic routes to reduce rutting and runoff concentration in convergent low positions.

## Climate History
- What this tab shows: Historical and seasonal climate context relevant to operations and risk.
- How to interpret: Use repeated timing patterns, not single events, to guide field planning.
- Decision support value: Supports realistic planting windows, harvest risk management, and resilient sequencing of conservation actions.
- Practical example: A manager identifies repeated late-spring wet periods and adjusts fieldwork order so poorly drained fields are planted later.

## Resource Concerns
- What this tab shows: A prioritized list of likely resource concerns synthesized from multiple evidence layers.
- How to interpret: Treat this as triage for planning; verify highest-priority concerns in the field.
- Decision support value: Speeds planning by translating many indicators into a concise intervention shortlist.
- Practical example: During a planning meeting, the team uses concern rankings to focus budget and labor on two high-impact issues first.

## Conservation Practices
- What this tab shows: Candidate conservation practices aligned to field conditions and resource concerns.
- How to interpret: Screen recommendations by feasibility, operation fit, cost, and expected localized benefit.
- Decision support value: Bridges analysis to implementation and supports phased rollout and cost-share planning.
- Practical example: A landowner builds a phased implementation plan, starting with high-benefit practices that have minimal operational disruption.

## Management Zones
- What this tab shows: Delineated management zones based on spatial variability in soils and productivity.
- How to interpret: Stable zone boundaries indicate strong candidates for differentiated management.
- Decision support value: Supports variable-rate prescriptions, targeted scouting, and zone-level economic tracking.
- Practical example: An operator applies three nitrogen rate zones, then evaluates response by zone to refine prescriptions next season.

## Suggested Delivery Format for End Users
For each tab in presentations, reports, or onboarding:
1. Start with what is being measured.
2. Explain how to read risk or opportunity in plain language.
3. Link directly to a management decision.
4. Close with one practical action example.

This sequence keeps technical detail grounded in operational decisions.
